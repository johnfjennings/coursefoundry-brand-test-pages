Tabletop Game Design, Prototype, Playtest, and Presentation

Author

Mr. John Jennings

Introduction

In this project, you will design an original tabletop game and develop it from initial concept through to prototype, playtest, refinement, and final presentation. Your task is to show not only that you can invent a game idea, but also that you can communicate rules clearly, build a playable prototype, collect useful feedback, improve your design, and explain your design decisions professionally.

This assignment combines concept development, prototype production, documentation, reflection, testing, and presentation into one complete design cycle. It is a full loop: you will move from idea, to physical object, to real player feedback, to a revised object, and finally to a professional explanation of the whole journey.

Project Goal and Core Requirements

The goal of this project is to create a simple, original tabletop game that can be explained clearly, played with minimal and accessible components, and improved through structured playtesting. Your final submission should demonstrate creativity, functional design, iterative development, and reflective thinking.

Your game must:

  • be an original design;
  • be playable by two players at minimum;
  • use accessible, low-cost, or easily substituted materials;
  • include a game board or main play area;
  • include clear rules and supporting components where needed;
  • be suitable for classroom playtesting;
  • show evidence of refinement based on feedback.

Project Stages

Although this is delivered as one assignment, you should work through it in the following sequence:

  1. Concept and design
  2. Prototype development
  3. Playtesting and feedback
  4. Refinement
  5. Final presentation and submission

Deliverable 1: Game Design Document

Submit one complete design document in Word or PDF format that includes:

  • Game title
  • Objective
  • Target number of players
  • Game concept summary
  • Game components
  • Setup instructions
  • Gameplay rules
  • Winning conditions
  • Core rules
  • Additional or special rules
  • Final game board or layout
  • Sketches or images of additional components
  • A short design reflection
  • A short refinement reflection explaining what changed after playtesting
  • A link to your blog post
  • A link to your video(s), if hosted online

Deliverable 2: Concept Board / Initial Layout

Your original game concept must begin within a 2 x A4 design constraint. This should include:

  • Page 1: concise game overview, objective, and basic rules
  • Page 2: board or main play layout

You may expand the game using additional components such as cards, counters, tokens, or other play materials, but the core concept must still be clearly visible in this original 2 x A4 format.

Deliverable 3: Prototype

Create a playable prototype of your game that includes:

  • board or main play surface;
  • all required components;
  • usable pieces, cards, tokens, dice, trackers, or substitutes;
  • a version of the game that can be tested by players.

The prototype does not need to be professionally manufactured, but it must be functional, readable, and suitable for live playtesting.

Deliverable 4: Playtest Evidence

Carry out at least one structured playtest with two participants. You must provide evidence of:

  • a playtest session;
  • player interaction with the game;
  • gameplay in progress;
  • issues, confusion points, or successes observed during play;
  • feedback gathered after the session.

When recording, focus on the game, the board, and the gameplay rather than participants’ faces, unless explicit permission has been given.

Deliverable 5: Video Components

You must produce two short video elements or one combined edited video that covers all of the following:

  • summary of the game and its rules;
  • explanation of setup and starting state;
  • demonstration of basic gameplay;
  • highlights from the playtest session;
  • summary of player feedback;
  • explanation of changes made after testing;
  • reflection on how the changes improved the game.

A combined video should be approximately 4–7 minutes in length. If submitted as separate videos, each should be concise and clearly labelled.

Deliverable 6: Blog Post

Publish a blog post documenting your project. This should include:

  • your concept development process;
  • key design decisions;
  • challenges encountered;
  • screenshots or photos of development;
  • embedded or linked video content;
  • reflection on what you learned through playtesting and refinement.

Deliverable 7: Final Presentation Component

Prepare and deliver a short presentation on your game. Create a 3–5 minute presentation supported by slides, a poster, or another teacher-approved visual format.

Your presentation must include:

  • the game title and concept;
  • intended player experience;
  • overview of mechanics;
  • prototype visuals;
  • key findings from playtesting;
  • the most important refinement(s) you made;
  • brief reflection on what you would improve next.

This presentation should be concise, visual, and clearly structured. It is intended to assess your ability to communicate your design process as well as the quality of the game itself.

Constraints and Guidance

  • The original concept must fit within the 2 x A4 requirement.
  • Additional components are allowed and encouraged where they improve gameplay.
  • Materials should be inexpensive, available, and practical.
  • Rules must be clear enough for another player to understand without your help.
  • The prototype should be functional rather than decorative only.
  • Playtesting should focus on learning from players, not defending the design.
  • Refinements should respond directly to observed issues or participant feedback.
  • You do not need to fully remake all components after playtesting, but your rule changes and design improvements must be clearly documented.

AI Usage

All submitted work must reflect your own design thinking, decision-making, and development process.

Acceptable use of AI tools. You may use AI tools to support:

  • spelling and grammar checking;
  • formatting assistance;
  • brainstorming prompts;
  • generating planning questions;
  • improving clarity of your writing.

Unacceptable use of AI tools. You may not use AI tools to:

  • generate the full game concept for you;
  • produce all rules without your own authorship;
  • invent fake playtest results or reflections;
  • generate blog reflections that do not match your actual process;
  • create fabricated design evidence;
  • produce presentation scripts that you cannot explain yourself.

Authenticity requirements. To support academic integrity, you must include:

  • at least three development checkpoints (photos, drafts, sketches, screenshots, or notes);
  • evidence of progression from initial idea to prototype to refined version;
  • brief annotations on what changed at each stage;
  • raw playtest notes or a feedback summary;
  • a short in-class or teacher check-in if requested;
  • the ability to explain and defend your own work during presentation or follow-up questioning.

If a student cannot explain submitted work, demonstrate their design decisions, or account for the development process, this may be treated as a breach of academic integrity.

Suggested Workflow

  1. Design your initial concept within the 2 x A4 constraint.
  2. Draft your rules and game structure.
  3. Create your first playable prototype.
  4. Record a short explanation of the game.
  5. Run a playtest with two players.
  6. Gather and organise feedback.
  7. Refine the game rules or mechanics.
  8. Update your documentation.
  9. Prepare your final blog post and video.
  10. Deliver your short presentation.

Submission Checklist

Submit the following:

  • Final game design document (Word or PDF)
  • Prototype evidence (photos and/or in-class presentation of prototype)
  • Playtest evidence
  • Video or videos
  • Blog post link
  • Final presentation slides or visual aid, if required by teacher

Integrated Marking Scheme

Your work is assessed out of 100 marks across seven criteria. For each, a mark band from 0–40%, 40–60%, 60–80%, and 80–100% describes the quality of your work, from unclear or missing evidence at the low end to polished, insightful, and highly effective work at the top.

  • Game Concept and Originality (15 marks): ranges from a concept that is unclear, generic, or heavily derivative, up to a concept that is highly inventive, distinctive, and immediately engaging.
  • Rules and Documentation Quality (15 marks): ranges from rules that are missing, unclear, or incomplete, up to rules that are precise, comprehensive, and can guide new players independently.
  • Board, Components, and Prototype Functionality (20 marks): ranges from a prototype that is missing or non-functional, up to a polished, coherent prototype with well-integrated components and strong usability.
  • Playtesting and Feedback Collection (15 marks): ranges from little or no meaningful playtest evidence, up to a thorough, well-run playtest with strong observations and high-quality feedback data.
  • Refinement and Iterative Improvement (15 marks): ranges from little change, or changes unrelated to feedback, up to targeted, well-justified refinements that clearly improve gameplay and show iterative thinking.
  • Reflection, Blog, and Process Evidence (10 marks): ranges from reflection that is missing or superficial with little process evidence, up to insightful, honest reflection with strong evidence of development across the project.
  • Presentation and Communication (10 marks): ranges from a presentation that is missing, unclear, or poorly structured, up to an engaging, polished presentation that communicates the full design journey effectively.

Notes on assessment:

  • Strong projects will show clear links between concept, prototype, playtest evidence, and final refinements.
  • Marks are awarded for process as well as final product.
  • Authentic evidence of development is essential.

Summary

This project takes you through a complete game design cycle: concept and design, prototype development, playtesting and feedback, refinement, and final presentation. You are required to produce seven deliverables — a game design document, a concept board within a 2 x A4 constraint, a playable prototype, playtest evidence, video components, a blog post, and a final presentation — each documenting a different stage of your design journey.

Throughout, your work must be your own: AI tools may support editing and brainstorming, but the concept, rules, playtest evidence, and reflections must reflect your genuine process, backed by development checkpoints and the ability to explain your decisions. Success is measured not only by the quality of the finished game but by clear evidence of iterative improvement — from initial idea, through honest playtesting, to justified refinement — communicated clearly in your documentation and final presentation.
